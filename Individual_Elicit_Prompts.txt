INDIVIDUAL ELICIT PROMPTS FOR DATA EXTRACTION
================================================

PROMPT 1 - Country
In which country was this study conducted? Provide the exact country name where the research took place. Look for explicit mentions of country names in the text, methodology section, study area description, or author affiliations. If the study covers multiple countries, list all countries separated by semicolons.

PROMPT 2 - City
In which city or cities was this study conducted? Provide only the exact city names mentioned in the study. Look for specific city names in the study area description, data collection section, or case study details. If multiple cities are included, list all cities separated by semicolons. Do NOT include counties, states, provinces, districts, or other regional identifiers - extract only the city names themselves.

PROMPT 3 - Name of the Spatial Unit
What is the exact name or type of spatial unit of analysis used in this study? Look for terms such as census tracts, census blocks, neighborhoods, wards, postal codes, ZIP codes, grid cells, super output areas, statistical districts, administrative areas, boroughs, precincts, beat areas, or any other geographic unit mentioned.

PROMPT 4 - Spatial Unit Size
What is the size or dimension of each individual spatial unit used in this study? Provide only the size measurement for a single unit (e.g., area of one census tract, size of one grid cell, etc.). If no specific unit size is mentioned, respond with "Not specified". Look for measurements like "each tract is X km²", "grid cells of X meters", "units averaging X hectares", etc. Do NOT include total study area, population counts, or aggregate measurements.

PROMPT 5 - Number of Units Used
How many spatial units were included in the final statistical analysis? Provide only the exact number. Look for this information in the methodology section, sample description, or results section.

PROMPT 6 - Rationale for Unit Size
What justification, reasoning, or explanation did the authors provide for choosing their specific spatial unit size or type? Look for discussions in the methodology section, study design, or spatial analysis sections. Include theoretical justifications, practical considerations, or references to previous research.

PROMPT 7 - SUoA Rationalization Category
Based on the rationale provided in the previous prompt, categorize the type of justification the authors used for their spatial unit of analysis (SUoA) selection. Choose the most appropriate category: "Previous research", "Theory", "Data availability", "Administrative convenience", "Theory and previous research", "Data availability and previous research", "Theory and data availability", or "Theory, previous research, and data availability".

PROMPT 8 - Data Limitation for Selection of Spatial Unit
What limitations, constraints, or factors influenced the choice of spatial units in this study? Look for data availability issues, administrative boundary constraints, privacy concerns, confidentiality requirements, aggregation requirements, statistical disclosure controls, or other factors that restricted the spatial unit selection.

PROMPT 9 - Data Collection Period
When was the data for this study collected? Provide the specific time period, dates, months, or years when the crime data or other study data was gathered. Look for phrases about when data was collected, accessed, obtained, downloaded, or when the study period occurred.

PROMPT 10 - Data Sources
What were the sources of data used in this study? List all data sources mentioned. Look for police databases, crime records, census data, administrative records, surveys, government agencies, or commercial databases. If multiple sources were used, separate them with semicolons.

PROMPT 11 - No of Data Sources Used
How many different data sources were used in this study? Provide the exact number of distinct data sources, databases, organizations, or agencies that provided data for the analysis.

PROMPT 12 - Average Population per Unit
What is the average population size per spatial unit in this study? Provide the average number of residents, inhabitants, or population per individual unit. If no average population is mentioned, respond with "Not specified".

PROMPT 13 - Total Study Area Size
What is the total area or size of the entire study region? Provide the overall geographic area covered by the study. If no total area is mentioned, respond with "Not specified". Look for measurements of the complete study area, city area, or regional coverage in square kilometers, square miles, hectares, etc.

PROMPT 14 - Whether They Aggregated the Data
Did the researchers aggregate, combine, or group data from smaller units to create larger analytical units? Provide "Yes" or "No" and describe the aggregation process if mentioned. Look for descriptions of how individual-level data was aggregated to area-level, or how smaller geographic units were combined into larger ones.

PROMPT 15 - Type of Crime
What specific type or category of crime does this study analyze? Look for crime types such as burglary, residential burglary, commercial burglary, theft, motor vehicle theft, robbery, street robbery, assault, drug offenses, vandalism, graffiti, fraud, or domestic violence. If multiple crime types are studied, list all of them separated by semicolons.

PROMPT 16 - Crime Type Group
Does this study analyze a single crime type or multiple crime types? Categorize the study as either "Single crime type" if only one specific crime is analyzed, or "Multiple crime types" if the study examines several different crime categories.

PROMPT 17 - Number of Crime Incidents
How many individual crime incidents or crime events were analyzed in this study? Provide the exact number of crime cases, incidents, events, or records that formed the basis of the statistical analysis.

PROMPT 18 - Discrete Choice Model
What type of discrete choice model was used in the statistical analysis? Look for conditional logit, multinomial logit, nested logit, mixed logit, random parameters logit, binary logit, probit, multinomial probit, or any other discrete choice modeling approach mentioned.

PROMPT 19 - Independent Variables
What are the independent variables, predictor variables, explanatory variables, or covariates used in the statistical model? Look specifically in model tables, regression tables, or statistical output tables for the complete list of variables. List all variables included in the regression analysis, choice model, or other statistical tests in a numbered format (1, 2, 3, etc.). Include both main variables of interest and control variables as they appear in the model tables.

PROMPT 20 - Total Number of Independent Variables
How many independent variables were included in the final statistical model? Provide the exact number of predictor variables, explanatory variables, or covariates used in the analysis.

PROMPT 21 - Model Fit Statistics
What model fit statistics, goodness-of-fit measures, or performance metrics are reported for the statistical model? Look for measures such as log-likelihood, pseudo R-squared, McFadden's R-squared, AIC, BIC, chi-square statistics, likelihood ratio tests, or other fit indices.

PROMPT 22 - Coefficients for All Variables
What are the coefficient values, parameter estimates, or beta values reported for each independent variable in the statistical model? Extract the specific numerical coefficient values for all variables. Include both significant and non-significant variables.

PROMPT 23 - Confidence Intervals
What confidence intervals are reported for the model coefficients or parameter estimates? Look for confidence interval values typically presented as ranges such as "95% CI: [lower bound, upper bound]" in results tables or statistical output.

PROMPT 24 - Effect Sizes
What effect sizes, odds ratios, relative risk ratios, or magnitude measures are reported for the independent variables? Look for odds ratios, hazard ratios, incidence rate ratios, Cohen's d, standardized coefficients, or percentage changes in outcome variables.

PROMPT 25 - Future Suggestions for Spatial Units
What future recommendations, suggestions, or improvements do the authors propose regarding spatial unit size or spatial unit of analysis? Look for discussions about alternative spatial units, different scales of analysis, optimal unit sizes, or methodological improvements related to spatial boundaries.

PROMPT 26 - Computational Constraints Mentioned
What computational limitations, memory constraints, or processing challenges (if any) did the authors mention that influenced their spatial unit selection? Provide detailed descriptions of any constraints mentioned such as memory limitations, processing time, computational feasibility concerns, or software limitations. If no constraints are mentioned, state "No computational constraints mentioned."

PROMPT 27 - Alternative Units Considered
What alternative spatial units or scales (if any) did the authors mention considering before selecting their final unit of analysis? Provide detailed descriptions of any alternative units that were discussed but not used, including reasons for rejection. If no alternatives are mentioned, state "No alternative units discussed."

PROMPT 28 - MAUP Discussion
How do the authors discuss the Modifiable Areal Unit Problem (MAUP) or ecological fallacy in relation to their spatial unit selection? Provide detailed descriptions of any discussion about scale effects, aggregation bias, or spatial autocorrelation issues. If MAUP is not discussed, state "No MAUP discussion present."

PROMPT 29 - Sensitivity Analysis
What sensitivity analysis (if any) did the study conduct using different spatial unit sizes or scales? Provide detailed descriptions of any comparison of results across different spatial scales, including methodological approaches and key findings. If no sensitivity analysis was conducted, state "No sensitivity analysis performed."

PROMPT 30 - Software Used
What statistical software, programming tools, or analytical platforms were used for the data analysis? Look for R, Stata, Python, SPSS, SAS, BIOGEME, Nlogit, ArcGIS, QGIS, Mplus, or any other analytical tools. Include version numbers if mentioned.
