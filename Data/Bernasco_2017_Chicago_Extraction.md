## Study Design & Methodology

### Research Question
**Main hypothesis**: "For street robbers, the importance of a target area attribute is conditional on the time of day and on the day of the week"

**Result**: **Hypothesis strongly rejected** - location preferences are mostly stable over time (except for high schools)

### Temporal Analysis
**Daily variation**: 12 separate models for two-hour blocks
- 4-6 a.m., 6-8 a.m., 8-10 a.m., 10 a.m.-noon, noon-2 p.m., 2-4 p.m., 4-6 p.m., 6-8 p.m., 8-10 p.m., 10 p.m.-midnight, midnight-2 a.m., 2-4 a.m.

**Weekly variation**: 7 separate models (one per day of week)
- Sunday through Saturday

**Total models estimated**: 19 models (12 time slots + 7 days)

### Temporal Patterns in Robbery Volume
- **Peak time**: Around 8 p.m.
- **Lowest time**: Around 6 a.m.
- **Low volume period**: 1 a.m. - 9 a.m. (when most people asleep)
- **High volume period**: 2 p.m. - 11 p.m.
- **Day with most robberies**: Friday
- **Day with least robberies**: Sunday

## Model Specification

### Model Type
- **Primary model**: Conditional logit (discrete choice model)
- **Theoretical basis**: Random utility maximization theory
- **Approach**: Discrete spatial choice

### Sampling of Alternatives Technique
- **Reason**: Reduce computational burden with 24,594 blocks
- **Sample size for daily models** (12 models): 11,999 alternative census blocks per robbery
- **Sample size for weekly models** (7 models): 7,999 alternative census blocks per robbery
- **Chosen block**: Always included + random sample of other blocks

### Bootstrap Estimation
- **Number of iterations**: 10
- **Purpose**: Obtain robust estimates and conservative standard errors
- **Combination method**: Rubin's (1987) formulae for combining bootstrapped regression estimates
- **Overall coefficient**: Average across iterations
- **Overall standard error**: Square root of summed within-iteration and between-iteration variances

### Model Decisions
- **No spatially lagged effects**: Decision made to use resources for temporal distinction rather than spatial complexity
- **Rationale**: Prior studies (Bernasco & Block 2011; Haberman & Ratcliffe 2015) show main effects much larger than lagged effects

### Distance Measure
- **Type**: Euclidean distance (not street network)
- **Justification**: "Chicago has few natural or man-made barriers to mobility and street network distance is very strongly correlated with Euclidian distance" (p. 257)

## Sensitivity Analysis

### Temporal Robustness
- **12 two-hour blocks**: Tested for daily variation
- **7 day-specific models**: Tested for weekly variation
- **Rationale for 2-hour blocks**: "Reasonable compromise between the need to use a fine-grained temporal measure on the one hand, and the need for statistical power in modeling and efficiency in presenting results on the other hand" (p. 258)
- **Considered but rejected**: One-hour blocks (would double number of models)

### Bootstrap Robustness
- **10 iterations** with different random samples
- Provides additional (between-iteration) variance in parameter estimates
- "More precise estimate of the parameters and a more conservative estimate of their standard errors" (p. 259)

### Co-offender Treatment
- Sensitivity noted: multioffender groups treated as single decision-making agent
- Justification: Bernasco (2006) showed co-offending and single offenders have same choice criteria

## Key Findings

### Main Result
**Hypothesis strongly rejected**: "For almost all census block attributes, their importance hardly depends on time of day or day of week" (p. 244)

### Effects That Remain Stable Over Time
**Nearly all 18 variables** show positive effects that don't vary significantly by time:
- Bars and clubs
- Restaurants and fast-food outlets
- Barbershops and beauty salons
- Liquor stores
- Grocery stores
- General merchandise stores
- Gas stations
- Laundromats
- Pawnshops and cash services
- Drug markets
- Prostitution areas
- Illegal gambling
- Main streets
- El stations
- Total population
- Distance from home

### Only Exception: High Schools
**Strong temporal variation**:
- **School hours** (6 a.m. - 4 p.m.): Statistically significant positive effects
- **Outside school hours**: All negative (non-significant)
- **Weekdays**: Positive effects
- **Weekends**: Not attractive when schools vacant

### Slight Temporal Variability
**Ethnic majority**: "Seem to prefer blocks with in-group majorities more during evenings and late nights than during mornings and afternoons" (p. 262) - but differences not statistically significant

### Comparison to Prior Research
**Similar to Haberman & Ratcliffe (2015) Philadelphia study**:
- Both studies found limited temporal variation in location preferences
- Both found most facilities attract crime throughout day
- High schools exception consistent across studies

## Theoretical Implications

### Theoretical Frameworks Discussed
1. **Crime pattern theory** (Brantingham & Brantingham 2008)
2. **Routine activity approach** (Cohen & Felson 1979)
3. **Time geography** (Hägerstrand 1970)
4. **Rational choice theory**

### Challenge to Theory
Results "seem to challenge the view that street robbers select utility-maximizing locations for committing robberies" (p. 265)

**Apparent contradiction**: "Robbers prefer blocks with grocers between 2 a.m. and 4 a.m., when the grocers are likely to be closed" (p. 265)

### Alternative Explanations Offered

1. **Model misspecification**: Missing facilities (e.g., ATMs operating 24/7) correlated with included facilities
   - Authors note: Haberman & Ratcliffe (2015) controlled for ATMs and still found limited variation

2. **Victims as guardians**: "Potential victims and their guardians are the same persons" (p. 266)
   - People on street are both targets and guardians
   - Numbers of victims and guardians always in balance

3. **Impulsive decision-making**: "Street robbery is generally not a premeditated activity, but an impulsive decision driven by opportunities that offenders are exposed to during their legitimate routine activities" (p. 266)
   - Offenders spend routine time in/around cash economies, illegal markets, transit facilities
   - Occasionally encounter opportunities that motivate robberies
   - If offenders not very mobile, crimes always in same places regardless of victim density

## Limitations & Future Directions

### Data Limitations

1. **Time as proxy**: "Did not actually measure each business' opening hours, measure the average number of people present per census block per time slot, or count numbers of vulnerable people on the streets" (p. 267)

2. **Missing facilities**: No data on ATMs, banks, drug treatment centers, neighborhood parks, public housing

3. **Cleared cases only**: 17.2% of all street robberies
   - But: Bernasco et al. (2013) showed this selection doesn't produce biased estimates

4. **Chicago residents only**: Excludes robberies by non-residents

5. **Business opening hours**: Unknown - assumed businesses closed outside normal hours

### Future Research Directions

1. **Direct measurement of human presence**: Use smartphones, location services, social media (Malleson & Andresen 2014)

2. **Temporal bandwidth sensitivity**: Test impact of different time aggregations (similar to MAUP)

3. **Other temporal cycles**: Annual/seasonal variation
   - Note: Andresen & Malleson (2013) found robbery "appears to occur in the same places regardless of the season of year" in Vancouver

4. **Network distance**: Use street network rather than Euclidean distance (though less important in Chicago)
